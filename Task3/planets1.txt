Mercury is the smallest planet in our Solar System and the closest to the Sun. It completes one orbit in just 88 Earth days, making it the fastest planet. Despite being close to the Sun, Mercury has extreme temperature swings: up to 430°C during the day and -180°C at night. It has no substantial atmosphere to retain heat.

Venus is often called Earth's "sister planet" due to similar size and mass. However, its surface is a hellish landscape with temperatures around 465°C. Venus has a thick atmosphere made mostly of carbon dioxide, creating a runaway greenhouse effect. It rotates in the opposite direction to most planets.

Earth is the only known planet to support life. It orbits the Sun at an average distance of 150 million kilometers, taking 365.25 days to complete one revolution. Earth's atmosphere contains 21% oxygen, and about 71% of its surface is covered by water. It has one natural satellite: the Moon.

Mars is known as the Red Planet because of iron oxide (rust) on its surface. It has the largest volcano in the Solar System, Olympus Mons, and a canyon system called Valles Marineris. Mars has two small moons: Phobos and Deimos. Scientists believe liquid water once flowed on its surface.

Jupiter is the largest planet in our Solar System, with a mass greater than all other planets combined. It is a gas giant composed mostly of hydrogen and helium. Jupiter has at least 95 known moons, including the four large Galilean moons: Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto. Its famous Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth.

Saturn is famous for its spectacular ring system, made of ice particles, rock, and dust. It is the least dense planet—less dense than water, meaning it would float if placed in a large enough ocean. Saturn has 146 confirmed moons, with Titan being the largest and the only moon in the Solar System with a substantial atmosphere.

Uranus is an ice giant with a blue-green color caused by methane in its atmosphere. It rotates on its side, with an axial tilt of about 98 degrees, likely due to a massive collision in its past. Uranus takes 84 Earth years to orbit the Sun and has 27 known moons.

Neptune is the windiest planet, with supersonic winds reaching up to 2,100 km/h. It is an ice giant similar to Uranus but more massive. Neptune was the first planet discovered through mathematical predictions rather than direct observation. It has 14 known moons, with Triton being the largest and geologically active.

Pluto was classified as the ninth planet from its discovery in 1930 until 2006. The International Astronomical Union reclassified it as a "dwarf planet" because it has not cleared its orbital neighborhood of other objects. Pluto has five known moons, with Charon being so large that the pair are sometimes considered a binary system.

The Sun contains 99.86% of the total mass of the Solar System. It is a G-type main-sequence star, about 4.6 billion years old. The Sun's energy comes from nuclear fusion in its core, where hydrogen atoms combine to form helium. This energy takes thousands of years to travel from the core to the surface, then just 8 minutes to reach Earth.
